The hard layouts in AI mockups, built to work on every screen

AI mockups keep reaching for the same five tricky layouts: overlapping photos, uneven grids, images that run off the edge, sticky columns and connected steps. Here's each one built with flex containers and CSS grid, the same structure I use in Elementor. Switch the screen size or turn on the structure view to see how they hold together.

Sample content uses Alder & Stone, a made-up garden studio. The green blocks are photo placeholders, labeled the way I'd mark them for your content.

Overlapping hero collage

Why it's hard
Two photos and a review card sit on top of each other and spill into the next section. Built with absolute positioning, it falls apart as soon as the screen gets narrower.
How it's built
A 6-column grid where the photos share cells, so they overlap but still flow with the page. The card uses a negative bottom margin, not fixed coordinates.
On phones
The second photo drops out and the card tucks under the main photo, so nothing covers the text.

Uneven tile grid

Why it's hard
Tiles of different sizes have to line up edge to edge. Fixed heights leave gaps the moment the text changes length.
How it's built
One 4-column grid. Tiles span 1 or 2 columns and rows, and their heights come from the content, not pixel values.
On tablets and phones
Drops to 2 columns, then 1. The big tile always stays first.

Photo that runs off the edge

Why it's hard
The photo runs to the edge of the screen while the text stays lined up with the rest of the page. Most builds either box the photo in or push the text out of line.
How it's built
A full-bleed grid with named edge and content columns. The photo spans from the screen edge to the center, and the stats bar overlaps its bottom edge.
On phones
The photo goes full width on top, with the stats in a row just under it.

Sticky side column

Why it's hard
The heading stays put while the questions scroll past. It quietly breaks if any parent section hides overflow, which page builders often add by default.
How it's built
Sticky positioning on the left column, with overflow left visible on every parent container.
On phones
Sticky turns off and the heading sits above the list.

What does a garden design cost?

Most design fees fall between $2,500 and $8,000, depending on size. The fee comes off your build if we install it.

Can you work with our home builder?

Yes. We line up grading, drainage and utilities with your builder so nothing gets dug up twice.

When is the best time to start?

Plan in winter, build in spring. Late-summer builds work well too and give plants a cooler start.

Do you handle permits?

We prepare and file permits for walls, patios and lighting wherever your town requires them.

How long does a build take?

Most gardens take three to six weeks on site once materials arrive.

What happens after the build?

Every garden gets a first-year visit schedule, and many clients move onto a seasonal care plan.

Connected process steps

Why it's hard
The line joining the steps has to stay lined up with the circles at every width, and usually ends up as a stretched image.
How it's built
A plain numbered list with the line drawn behind it in CSS, so you can add or remove a step without redrawing anything.
On phones
It turns into a vertical timeline with the line running down the left.
